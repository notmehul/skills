# Cold email

The reader owes you nothing and decides from the preview. The email earns a reply by being about them and their problem, not about you.

## Before writing

Have these three, or ask for them:
- **Why them:** a specific, sourced, dated observation about this person or their company.
- **The ask:** the one thing you want (a reply, a short call, an intro).
- **The proof:** one thing you can actually show.

If the project has `docs/positioning.md`, read it. If the person is already in the network, run `relationship-brief` first; a cold template sent to a warm contact is worse than silence.

## The email

- **Hook.** An observation about them that ties to the problem you solve. Read it as them and ask "so what?". A compliment, a funding congrats, or "saw your post" with no link to the problem is an attention grab; cut it.
- **Body.** The problem in their terms, then one proof point you can back. One, not a feature list.
- **Ask.** One ask, a verb plus what they get: "reply yes and I'll send the two-page teardown", "20 minutes Thursday to run it on your repo". The more senior the reader, the smaller the ask. A first touch asks for a reply, not a calendar slot.
- **Length.** Readable on a phone without scrolling. Plain text, at most one link, no images.
- **Subject.** Two to four words, lowercase, like a colleague wrote it: "onboarding drop-off", "your mcp server". No first name, no pitch, no fake "Re:".
- **Register.** Founders and investors get the peer version: shortest, the ask in one line. Researchers and academics get the specific paper or project, and why you in particular are writing.
- **Voice.** Under Mehul's name, run mehul-voice (founder and investor register), then unslop. For a Biome artifact, also follow Biome's drafting rules: no contractions, no em dashes, "USD" instead of the symbol. Numbers follow unslop rule 32.

## Follow-ups

- At most two, and each adds something new: a different angle, a fresh proof point, a useful artifact. A follow-up with nothing new in it doesn't get sent.
- Each one stands alone; assume they never read the earlier ones.
- Space them out: a few days, then a week or two.
- The last one closes the loop and means it: "I'll stop here. If <problem> becomes a priority, reply any time." Record any "no" or "not now" as an open loop in relationship-brief and honour it.

## Before sending

Read it as the recipient on a phone. Would you reply? Is every sentence about them or their problem? Is there exactly one ask? Is every number sourced? Fix whatever fails, then hand it over.
