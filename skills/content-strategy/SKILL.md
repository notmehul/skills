# Content strategy

Content that brings the right people to you is something useful they keep, plus the argument that makes it worth keeping. Plan the artifact first, then the writing around it.

## Steps

1. **Pin the job.** Who should come inbound (founders in a sector, operators, investors, users), and what should they do next (reply, book a call, share the artifact, sign up)? Read `docs/positioning.md` if the project has one. Done when you can fill in: "<audience> who <situation> get <artifact>, so they <next step> with us."
2. **Find topics from evidence.** Start from the questions people already bring you (DMs, calls, portfolio founders), then what the audience argues about in public (customer-research, mining mode), then gaps in what the benchmark publishers put out. Every topic carries the evidence that it matters.
3. **Score and pick.** Score each topic from 1 to 5 on four things, weighting the first two highest: how often the audience hits this problem (from step 2), how much only we can say about it (our data, access, portfolio), how cheaply we can make it well, and how directly it leads to the next step. Make the top one to three and drop the rest.
4. **Design the artifact.** It solves one specific problem in one format, and it's usable within five minutes of opening.
   - Spreadsheet: a "How to use" tab, example data already filled in, input cells kept apart from locked formulas, a sources tab.
   - Template or sample deck: placeholders in `[brackets]` and one worked example.
   - Sector map or teardown: dated, a source on every claim, and what it means for the reader on the first page.
   Gate it behind a form only when the next step genuinely needs contact details; for a small team, ungated reach is usually worth more. Every artifact ends on the one next step.
5. **Write the flagship piece and make it citable.** Alongside any original research, publish the findings as a short list of one-line, sourced statements others can quote and link. In one vendor study (Foundation Inc., March 2026, B2B SaaS sites; treat it as directional), curated statistics pages earned far more links than the original research behind them. That page is also what AI answers lift.
6. **Build it to be distributed.** Write subheads that stand alone as posts, charts that work without the article, and quotes worth pulling. Send it to owned channels first (site, newsletter, email list), then borrowed ones (someone else's newsletter or podcast), then rented ones (LinkedIn, X). Posts under Mehul's name go through mehul-voice.
7. **Set a cadence you can hold at the bar.** For example, one flagship piece a month and two short pieces derived from it. If a piece misses the bar, skip the slot rather than ship it.

## Output

A plan table, one row per piece:

| Topic | Evidence it matters | Artifact | Reader's next step | Channels | Owner, date |

Measure it by replies, meetings, and shares from the right people, and record which artifact each inbound came through. Page views don't count.
