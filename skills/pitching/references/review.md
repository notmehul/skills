# Reviewing a pitch

The five audit layers with their signals, the anti-pattern library with rewrites, and the hot-cold scoring guide for branch 2 of `pitching`. For every hit: quote the text, name the violation, write the rewrite.

## Layer 1: neediness

Validation-seeking signals weakness to the croc brain. Flag on sight:

- "What do you think?", "Do you think this is good?"
- "I hope this works for you", "I hope you'll consider"
- "We'd love the opportunity to", "We'd be grateful for the chance to"
- "Please let me know if you have any questions"
- "Just checking in", "Just following up", "Sorry to bother you"
- "We can be flexible on" when nobody asked; "We can sign right away if you want"
- "Let me know what works for your schedule"

Flag and explain: qualifiers (maybe, perhaps, I think, sort of), an apologetic tone anywhere, unprompted discounts, multiple exclamation marks, explaining why you are worthy instead of showing it, more information than was asked for, repeated follow-ups that add nothing, letting the other party set the terms, timeline and format.

Rewrite with the anti-neediness formula: want nothing, show one thing you do well, withdraw. "I'd love the opportunity to discuss how we might help your team. Do you have 15 minutes this week?" becomes "We're solving [specific problem] for companies like [peer]. I have 15 minutes Thursday at 2. Does that work?"

## Layer 2: frame

Who is reacting to whom? The sender has lost the frame when the text reacts to the recipient's priorities, borrows their language, explains defensively ("The reason we...", "To clarify..."), accommodates unasked, positions itself below the recipient, or explains credentials at length. The sender holds the frame when it sets the agenda, timeline and terms, makes the recipient react, asserts without qualifiers, positions itself as the prize, and carries time constraints that signal demand.

Rewrite so the recipient qualifies themselves. "We have extensive experience in this space and believe we could add significant value" becomes "We're selective about partnerships. From what I've seen there could be a strong fit, but I'd need to understand more about how your team operates."

## Layer 3: tension and attention

Flat text reads as a feature list, has no push or pull, never introduces risk, never takes anything away, is entirely positive, could be an encyclopaedia entry, or front-loads everything with nothing held back. Add a push-pull loop at the right intensity (the library is in `build.md`), a novelty injection (an unexpected comparison, a counterintuitive number, a break in tone), and an intrigue element (a story with stakes and an unresolved ending, an information gap).

"Our platform integrates with 50+ tools, offers real-time analytics and has 200 enterprise customers" becomes "When [company] tried to solve this last year they burned through $2M before calling us. I'll tell you what happened, but first the thing that matters: we built something their existing 50 tools couldn't."

## Layer 4: hot versus cold cognition

People decide they want something before they understand it; the data is collected to justify the decision. Humans cannot hold hot cognitions (desire) and cold cognitions (analysis) at the same time, and once the analytical brain is running it is hard to switch back. Cold traps: opening with statistics, ROI as the primary argument, feature comparison tables, long technical explanations, a problem > data > solution > ask structure.

Fix: emotion first, logic second. The four hot triggers are intrigue, prize positioning, a time constraint with a checkable reason behind it, and moral authority ("We do things right. Can you?").

### Scoring hot versus cold, 1 to 10

| Score | What it looks like |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 to 2 | Leads with data. No narrative, no hook. Reads as a spec sheet; the croc brain drops it within 30 seconds |
| 3 to 4 | Data-driven with emotional language bolted on |
| 5 to 6 | Alternates; neither dominates; does not clearly lead with desire |
| 7 to 8 | Opens with narrative, intrigue or social proof; data arrives after engagement |
| 9 to 10 | The full stack: intrigue, prize, time, moral authority, with cold data only as validation of a decision already made |

### Restructuring template, for anything under 7

1. Open with intrigue or a personal story with stakes and an unresolved ending (hot).
2. Establish prize dynamics: selective, in demand, hard to get (hot).
3. Introduce the idea with minimum threat, in the idea introduction pattern (warm).
4. Build tension with push-pull loops (hot).
5. Now the data, high-level, as justification; details to the appendix (cold, supportive).
6. Close with a real time frame and a values check (hot).

Then one last neediness pass. Cut any sentence that asks for validation, uses "hope", "would love", "just" or "maybe", offers an unprompted concession, apologises for the follow-up, or expresses gratitude for the opportunity. Replace with sentences that state facts, set terms, make the sender's time scarce, make the reader qualify, and end in withdrawal rather than pursuit. In writing the formula reads: state what is available and let them come; lead with one proof point; close by pulling away ("If this isn't the right time, no worries. We'll be fully committed by [date] regardless", only with a real date).

## Layer 5: structure (longer pieces)

Check against the 20-minute shape: time frame set (0 to 2), one-item track record (2 to 4), why now with three forces (4 to 5), idea introduction (5 to 6), budget, secret sauce and competition with tension loops (6 to 15), the deal (15 to 17), the hot-cognition stack (17 to 20), the anti-neediness close. Common faults: no time frame at the open, a background section over 2 minutes or over one accomplishment, no why-now (the idea feels timeless), no tension in the middle, no stack at the end, a close that asks for validation.

## Anti-pattern library

### Neediness

- **The grateful opener.** "Thank you so much for taking the time to meet with us today." Puts the sender beneath the recipient. Rewrite: "Good to see you. I've got about 20 minutes before my next meeting, so let's get into it."
- **The eager closer.** "We'd love to move forward whenever you're ready. We can be flexible on timing and terms." Wanting, their pace, an unprompted concession. Rewrite: "We're committing resources to this in Q2. If you want in, let's lock terms by Friday."
- **The apologetic follow-up.** "Sorry to follow up again, just wanted to check if you'd had a chance to review." With a real constraint: "Our pilot cohort starts on the 30th and has two slots left. Tell me by Thursday if you want one." Without one: "One new thing since we spoke: [result or change]. I have Thursday at 2 to walk through it." Never invent the deadline; if the sender could not prove the constraint, use the second form.
- **The over-explainer.** A paragraph on why you are reaching out. If you have to explain why someone should talk to you, you have lost the frame. Rewrite: "You're expanding into Europe. We've taken three companies your size through that regulation in the last year. Worth 15 minutes?"
- **The discount volunteer.** "We could also look at a reduced scope to fit your budget." Remove. If they ask about budget: "Our pricing reflects the results we deliver. Let's talk about what success looks like first."

### Frame loss

- **The reactive proposal.** "Per your request, here is the proposal in the format you specified." Their request, their format, their sections. Rewrite: "Here's our proposal. We've structured it around the three things that decide whether this works." Then use your structure.
- **The authority explainer.** "With over 15 years of experience and household-name clients, we have the track record to deliver." Reads as "please believe I'm qualified". Rewrite: "When [one client] had this exact problem, we fixed it in 6 weeks. Here's what we'd do for you."
- **The committee accommodator.** "Happy to present to whoever you think should be involved, whenever suits." Hands over audience, time and format. Rewrite: "I'd like to present to whoever makes the final call. I have Thursday at 2 or Friday at 10. Which works?"
- **The scope-creep acceptor.** "Sure, we can add that." Soft boundaries, inflated pricing. Rewrite: "We can do that. It changes the scope, so I'll send revised terms by end of day."

### Flat content

- **The feature list.** Zero tension; filtered as boring information. Rewrite: "Most analytics platforms give you dashboards. We give you decisions. One client caught a $2M revenue leak in week one that their previous tools missed."
- **The positive-only pitch.** "Tremendous opportunity, massive potential, perfectly aligned." No threat of loss, so attention flatlines. Rewrite: "The window is open now and won't stay open. Two well-funded competitors are 6 months behind us. Do we use the lead or let it evaporate?"
- **The data-dump opener.** Market size, CAGR, TAM, SAM in the first breath. Cold cognition, and hard to recover from. Rewrite: open with the three converging forces.
- **The encyclopaedia section.** "AI has been advancing rapidly in recent years." Generic context kills novelty. Cut it. If context is needed, make it surprising.

### Structure

- **The résumé opening.** Founded in, team from [three famous employers], 20 years of experience. Impression is the average of the items, and it is cold. Rewrite: one impressive thing the founder built and how it applies here.
- **The no-time-frame opening.** "We've prepared a comprehensive presentation covering..." The croc brain hears "trapped". Rewrite: "I've got 20 minutes to give you the big idea, then we'll talk."
- **The missing why-now.** Straight from background to product. Insert the three forces.
- **The validation-seeking close.** "What do you think? Any questions? We'd love your feedback." Three neediness signals in a row. Rewrite: "That's the opportunity. We're committing fully in [timeframe] with or without outside participation. Tell me about your team; I need to know if this is the right fit."

### Email

- **The subject line that begs.** "Quick question", "Touching base", "Following up (3rd time)". Rewrite: "Q2 partnership: decision needed by Friday" (with a real Friday) or "[Specific result] in [timeframe]".
- **The wall of text.** Six paragraphs before the point. Cold outreach is 3 to 5 sentences; the point is sentence one.
- **The multiple-questions email.** Three questions, three decisions, inaction. Rewrite: "I have Thursday at 2. Worth 15 minutes?"

## The score card

Rate 1 to 10, one sentence of evidence each: neediness (1 desperate, 10 none), frame ownership (1 fully reactive, 10 in control), tension and attention (1 flat, 10 push-pull throughout), hot cognition (the table above), structure (1 no framework, 10 the 20-minute shape; longer pieces only).
