# Public web pages: the idea, the words, the pictures, the measure

Read this when the UI is a landing page, marketing site, portfolio, product page, one-pager or hero. For a full build, run the installed `design-taste-frontend` skill: it deals a researched direction, searches open collections for real pictures, and measures the rendered page in a real browser. This sheet carries the rules and the numbers so they hold when you build a single section, review someone else's page, or cannot run the scripts.

Where the numbers come from: a study of 247 One Page Love and Awwwards sites captured 5 Oct 2026 and judged by model reviewers from screenshots (taste-skill v2, MIT, read 18 Oct 2026). Two galleries, model judges, fashion as much as quality, and the skill built on it did not beat a no-skill baseline on juror score. Treat the strong-versus-weak numbers as priors and the house bans as one studio's taste; the three bans its own data contradicts (monospace labels, index numbering, hairlines) are left out of this sheet.

## 1. The idea comes before the style

A page built from defaults looks like the average of every page: a centred headline over three lines, a label above it, a row of icon cards, a soft gradient. The study's panel scored lowest the sites whose idea was "none beyond the standard formula", and the pages that won blind tests were built from something only that client has.

1. **Inventory the subject**: eight to twelve things that belong to this client and to no competitor. Its name and what it means; its street and what that place looks like; the numbers in the brief (seats, rooms, the price, the year); the constraint it lives with (one batch a month, closed in winter); its materials and colours; its rituals and hours; the printed things of its world (a timetable, a label, a ticket).
2. **Write the idea sentence** and do not build until both blanks come from the inventory: "On this page, [something that belongs to the subject] becomes [the device the composition calls for]." Places to look: its output, its object or silhouette, its name, its place, its ritual, its artefact, its opposite.
3. **The competitor test**: put the nearest real competitor in the sentence. If it still works, the idea belongs to the category. "Clean and modern" is not an idea.
4. **Pick the direction against the inventory, not by comfort.** A model asked to choose a direction returns the category default every time; have a script deal three (the installed skill does) or write three unlike compositions down and take the one that can carry something only this client has. Nine idea engines seen on the 7.3+ sites: the product demonstrates itself; the page is an artefact from the subject's world; the name is the hero; one motif from the subject drives everything; dressed as another category; the place carries it; a fixed stage; one section, one colour; radical understatement.
5. **One live thing**: one thing the visitor can do, or that differs each visit, made from the idea (pick a wood and see the chair in it; set the loan and read the real monthly figure). It uses real content, works with a thumb on a phone, has a plain fallback without script, never blocks reading, and is one thing, not a playground.

## 2. The words

Copy is the first design decision. A headline that needs four lines cannot be rescued by typography.

**The argument.** Answer in one line each: who is reading and what they already know; what this is, said the way its maker would say it to a friend; the one thing the reader should believe after one screen; what would make them believe it; what they do next. Then list the page as claims, one per section, each with its proof ("Each seat is cut from one board, so the grain is yours → three close-ups"). A section with no claim is filler. The order of the argument is the order of the page, which is how you escape hero, logos, features, testimonial, pricing, call to action.

**The two-line headline.** Every headline, at every width, fits in two lines or fewer. The arithmetic: characters per line ≈ line width in px ÷ (font size in px × k), with k ≈ 0.40 for condensed faces, 0.50 for most grotesks and serifs at display size, 0.58 for wide faces and all caps.

| Where | Line width | Size | Per line | Two-line budget |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Phone 390, 20px gutters | 350px | 44px | ~16 | ~32 characters |
| Phone 390, 20px gutters | 350px | 34px | ~21 | ~42 characters |
| Tablet 768 | 690px | 64px | ~22 | ~44 characters |
| Desktop, 12-column measure | 1100px | 104px | ~21 | ~42 characters |
| Desktop, half width | 620px | 72px | ~17 | ~34 characters |

So a hero headline of about 32 characters (four to six words) survives everywhere at a confident size; the gallery median is 5 words. Give it a `max-width` in `ch` and `text-wrap: balance`; author the break with `<br>` where it matters and check the phone; never fix a long headline only by shrinking it (nine timid words at 28px lose to five at 64px); rebalance an orphan by cutting a word. Section headings: two to five words.

**A statement is not a headline.** An intro sentence or quotation set large is a `<p>` or `<blockquote>` of about 25 words at statement size (36px or less on desktop, 32 on a tablet, 24 on a phone) and may run three lines (four on a phone). Larger, or marked up as a heading, or the main line of the first screen, it is a headline and gets two lines. Jurors read a big sentence over three lines as a long headline every time.

**Shapes that work**: the name, set very large; what it is ("Furniture for small rooms"); the claim a competitor could not sign ("Your bank won't do this"); the instruction, verb first ("Understand your money"); the place or the moment ("Lisbon, since 1984"). **Shapes to avoid**: the mad-lib ("The AI-powered platform for modern teams"); the tricolon ("Plan. Build. Ship."); the correction ("not just a tool, it's a workflow"); "X, reimagined"; "Welcome to"; a question nobody asked; a headline that needs its colon; one word styled differently. Sentence case.

**Supporting line**: one sentence, at most about 22 words, concrete, never the headline in other words. **Body**: the specific noun and the real number ("ships in a week", "38 seats"); if another company could print the sentence unchanged, cut it. **Buttons**: name the action or the thing, one to three words, the primary action keeps one name across the page, one primary per screen, the second action a text link, at most one arrow glyph. **Navigation**: the wordmark, up to five single-noun links, one action.

**Proof is never invented**: no customers, named quotes, press logos, user counts, ratings or benchmarks. When none exists, show the thing itself large, state how it works, use the brief's facts. **Sample details are different and expected**: where the brief gives the shape of content but not every detail (six rooms "from €140"), write the full plausible set so the design is seen at real density, and list every sample in the report for the client to replace.

**Reads as generated**: unlock, unleash, supercharge, elevate, empower, streamline, seamless, effortless, robust, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, all-in-one, blazing fast; editorial labels ("Field notes", "Quietly trusted by"); furniture text (locale and time strips, "Est. 2024", coordinates, version stamps, "Scroll to explore", fake photo credits, "Available for work" with a dot, middle-dot strings); the em dash as a habit; captions that narrate the design; step labels that duplicate numbers; three parallel items everywhere.

**Micro-typography**: curly quotes and apostrophes, a real ellipsis, an en dash for ranges; a non-breaking space between a number and its unit and before the last short word of a headline; `tabular-nums` where numbers align; `text-wrap: balance` on headings and `pretty` on paragraphs; no all caps past three words and 0.02 to 0.06em tracking when you do; nothing under 13px; no centred paragraph over three lines; no justified text.

## 3. Type on a page

What 202 measured sites do: hero headline median 72px (5.0vw; top sites 6.0vw), 40px on a phone; largest type on the page median 6.4vw, top sites 8.4vw against 5.0vw on the weak ones; hero tracking -0.01em (-0.03 to 0), leading 1.0 (0.95 to 1.1); body 16px; headline ÷ body 4.3 (top sites 4.7); typefaces in use: one 31%, two 45%, three 22%; hero weight regular 48%, medium 18%, bold or heavier 22%. Size contrast is the lever; weight is not.

- **Few sizes, far apart**: small (13 to 14px), body (17 to 19px), lead (20 to 26px), heading (32 to 64px), display (set by the composition). Five is the ceiling; display at least four times body on desktop; nine sizes near each other read as mush; if two sizes look almost the same, make them the same.
- **Display type** (anything over 40px): `line-height: 0.95` (0.85 to 1.05, tighter for capitals and condensed faces), `letter-spacing: -0.035em` (-0.02 to -0.05em, tighter as size grows, set per step), `font-kerning: normal`, `text-wrap: balance`, a `max-width` in `ch`. Leading under 1.0 needs a descender collision check. All caps want 0 to +0.03em and leading about 0.9. Pull a headline that starts with O, C, A, T or W left by 0.04 to 0.06em so it aligns optically. No text shadow, outline stroke or gradient fill on display type.
- **Text**: measure 45 to 75 characters (`max-width: 62ch`); body leading 1.45 to 1.6, lead 1.3 to 1.4; `p + p` spaced by about 0.9em; secondary text is the same face in a softer ink, not a lighter weight and never under 13px; no centred paragraphs past three lines; no justification; light on dark drops a weight step or adds 0.01em and a little leading.
- **Fonts**: request only the weights you use (two or three in total); a fallback stack of the same kind; `font-display: swap` or the framework loader; after building, confirm the declared face actually rendered, because a fallback makes every screenshot judgement false.
- **One family or two**: a third of strong sites use one, the safest way to be coherent. With two, each has a job (one sets headlines, the other everything else) and they never swap; no third face for labels, numbers or quotes; no two faces inside one headline; italic for titles of works and real emphasis, never decoration.
- **Details that separate set type from typed text**: `text-underline-offset: 0.18em; text-decoration-thickness: 0.07em`; `hyphens: none` on headings; space above a heading at least twice the space below; `::selection` and `:focus-visible` in the accent; never change weight on hover (the line reflows).
- **What a meter cannot see**: a headline that fits two lines but breaks badly (author the break); display type so tight letters touch; section headings almost as large as the hero (one dominant size per screen); body set in a display-only face; everything bold.

## 4. Colour on a page

Study: 41% dark, 59% light; buttons neutral (black, white, grey) on 52% of sites; gradient fills on 22% of top sites against 52% of weak ones, gradient text 2% against 13%; resting shadows 24% against 48%; one site in 246 has a terracotta primary button and none a violet one; top sites change the field colour between sections more often (median two grounds against one).

- **Roles**: `--bg`, `--surface` (one tone step from the ground), `--ink`, `--muted` (still passes contrast on `--bg`), `--accent`, `--accent-ink` (text on the accent), and `--field-n` with its own ink where sections change ground. A raw hex in a component is a second palette starting; do not add greys between the roles.
- **Proportion**: ground 70 to 90% of the page; accent under 5% of any screen, on the action and at most one other recurring thing; in five places it stops meaning "here". The accent is not for headings, icons, bullets, borders or underlines. A headline is ink.
- **Surfaces**: told apart by tone alone, 2 to 6 lightness points from the ground; one step is enough; no panel inside a panel; images need no frame; a thick bar (4px or more) in ink or accent is a graphic element, a 1px line is not.
- **Fields**: meet edge to edge with a hard line; every field has its own ink, never `--muted` on a coloured field; neighbours differ in lightness, not only hue; order them like chapters; the theme colour and the navigation follow the first field.
- **Dark pages**: `color-scheme: dark` on the root; a deep tinted ground and a soft light ink rather than #fff on #000; check `--muted` at 4.5:1; depth from a lighter surface step, never a glow; lighter weights.
- **Photographs**: the interface stays neutral and takes its ink from the darkest tone in the imagery; no brand-colour wash; text over a photograph needs a calm region or a neutral scrim (black or the ink, 30 to 55% opacity, over the text area only), never a coloured overlay.
- **Contrast**: body 4.5:1 or better, display and large numerals 3:1, the palette's ink on ground 7:1; never lower text opacity to make it "soft" without checking; focus and hover raise contrast, never reduce it; a body set in soft grey reads unfinished.
- **The generated palettes**: yellow-cream ground with terracotta, rust or clay accent (either half alone is suspect); violet and indigo. Warmth comes from a low-chroma stone, bone or putty ground (saturation under about 15%), a deep green, brown or oxblood ink, photographs, or a full warm field used flat. Warmth is not beige. If the brand owns one of these colours, keep it and say so.

## 5. Layout

What strong pages do: outer margins of 32px or less (about 45 of 114; only about 8 use 80px or more; the centred 1100 to 1300px container is the minority among strong sites and the usual frame of the ones called stock); text hanging from one off-centre anchor line; two or three type sizes with nothing between; one thing per screen; sections told apart by their ground; sections recomposed, not repeated; the system broken once on purpose; often no navigation bar; few words (median 575, some of the best under 200).

- **Margins and grid**: one pair of margins (16 to 32px) and one 12-column grid for the whole page; large things run to the edges; constrain only the measure of running text, in `ch` on the paragraph, not on the page; cap the layout at about 1760 to 1920px on very wide screens.
- **Anchor lines**: pick one off-centre line and reuse it for the hero headline, the section headings, the price, the footer links. Two or three on a whole page; six means nothing was decided. Centred is a decision: one axis, wide empty flanks, a single element per screen, not a centred stack of heading, paragraph and two buttons.
- **Empty space is placed**: the gap between sections clearly larger than any gap inside one (120 to 220px desktop, 72 to 112px phone); a heading has at least twice the space above it as below; when a section looks empty, make the main element bigger or cut the section, almost never "add something".
- **Sections**: the idea survives to the last pixel, pricing and FAQ included; no two neighbours share a layout (count the shapes in the screenshot; three of one shape is the template showing); alternate dense and empty, image and type, light and dark; a repeated module is for archives only; break the system once; short pages are fine.
- **Navigation**: the wordmark, three to five text links, one action, at text size on the page ground with no bar; fixed navigation never sits on content at any scroll position; on a phone, wordmark and one action in view; it takes at most a tenth of the first screen on desktop and one row on a phone. A nav that ate the hero cost a whole point of usability in blind tests.
- **Endings**: a designed scene (the name at full width, the hero inverted, one contact fact as a headline, one quiet line); link-column footers appear in the panel's notes only as criticism; practical facts in the text face at a readable size.
- **The phone is a second composition**: design 390px as its own page; re-author line breaks; re-crop to portrait per breakpoint; margins 16 to 20px; body 16 to 18px; targets 44px or taller; sideways scroll and three-line headlines appear here first.

## 6. Imagery

Study: on 60% of sites one piece of media covers at least half the first screen (top 68%, weak 48%); only 15% of heroes have no media; media has square corners on 73% of sites, otherwise one radius of about 12px applied alike; top pages carry a median of 37 images against 5 on weak ones. Panel scores by imagery kind: video and type-only highest (7.5), illustration-led lowest (6.9).

- **Preference order**: the project's own images; images generated this session (prompt as a photographer's brief: subject, angle, light, background, crop, "no text"; hero first, the rest matched to its light); real pictures of the subject's world from open collections (Openverse, Wikimedia Commons, the Art Institute of Chicago, the Met, all keyless; the installed skill's `assets.mjs` searches them); pictures drawn in code from the subject itself; generic atmosphere photographs only where an honestly generic picture fits; type and colour alone. Pages that drew their subject with care beat pages that borrowed unrelated photographs every time.
- **Search the subject's world, not the category**: its place in old postcards, its maps, its materials close up, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind, scientific plates; in the place's own words, with a medium or a decade. A good find belongs to this subject and would not fit the competitor's page, reads at the crop, is at least twice the displayed width for full bleed, belongs to one family with the others (all engravings, or one era, or one light), and carries no watermark, burned-in text, scanner edge or recognisable living person.
- **Honesty**: a found picture is never the client's own product, room, dish, person, team or customers; caption from the source, never tying it to the client. **Licences**: CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA; NC and ND are out for a client site; CC BY-SA alterations are shared alike; download, never hotlink; credit title, creator, source link and licence link in one footer line at body size; list every picture in the report.
- **Cropping and placing**: a small set of ratios (4:5, 16:10, 1:1); go large, one image at full width beats six small ones; `object-fit: cover` with an `object-position` chosen per image; let images touch edges; at most one overlap per screen, fixed 40 to 80px; square corners unless the direction says otherwise; no frames, borders, shadows, device mock-ups or browser chrome; captions one short line in the small size, left-aligned to the image, most images need none.
- **One treatment for the page**: untouched, or greyscale (`grayscale(1) contrast(1.05)`) on every image, or multiply on a flat field, or `brightness(0.85) contrast(1.05)` on a dark page. Not treatments: colour washes, duotone, blur, grain, vignettes, tilted frames.
- **Showing software**: a real fragment of the interface in HTML at true size with believable content (real labels, numbers that add up, one row hovered), cropped to the part that makes the point, on the page or one flat stage colour, no laptop frame; grey bars standing in for text are worse than no image. Inside the fragment the product's own conventions apply (small radii, 1px control borders, a shadow on an open menu); mark its root `data-ui` and keep its CSS under `[data-ui]` so the page rules stop at its edge. Real screenshots are shown large and uncropped at least once.
- **Drawing the subject**: the subject's own things, never decoration; one graphic language in the palette's real colours; three levels of detail (big shapes at a glance, middle shapes that describe, small details that reward a second look), because jurors called simple drawings "basic" every time; light from flat tone steps, not gradients; drawn large; say in small text that pictures are drawn until photographs exist. Not: gradient blobs, isometric clip art, people at laptops, floating shapes, icons in squares.
- **Type and colour alone** is a legitimate design (7 sites, median 7.5): one statement at 10 to 14vw, a list at 5vw, numerals at display size, a full field. Never an empty box where a picture "will go".
- **Markup**: `width` and `height` always; `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero and `loading="lazy"` elsewhere; `decoding="async"`; alt in plain words, `alt=""` for decorative repeats; a ground-coloured `background-color` on the container so the layout is whole before the image arrives; a broken image is a failure.
- **Video**: 5 to 20 second loops that close without a jump; under about 5 MB per section and 10 MB for a hero, 720p is plenty behind type; no slate, title card, logo or watermark; one per page, two at most; `autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata" poster width height aria-hidden="true"`; the poster works as a picture on its own; under reduced motion remove autoplay, pause and `load()`; never under body text; a headline over a clip gets the same scrim.

## 7. Motion on a page

Four questions first: how often will someone see it (once per visit can take its time; many times, fast and small; hundreds of times, none); what is it for (where something came from, confirming an action, keeping a change from jarring, explaining the product, the signature moment; "it looks nice" is valid only for the signature moment); which easing (entering or leaving ease-out, so movement starts the instant the eye arrives; travelling while visible ease-in-out; hover colour `ease`; constant travel `linear`; never `ease-in`, its first frames barely move); how long (press 100 to 160ms, hover 150 to 200, menus 150 to 250, dialogs and drawers 250 to 450, section and hero reveals 500 to 900, leaving about 70% of arriving).

- **Tokens**: `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`, `--ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)`, `--ease-soft: cubic-bezier(0.33, 1, 0.68, 1)`; `--t-press 140ms`, `--t-hover 180ms`, `--t-ui 240ms`, `--t-reveal 800ms`. Name the properties; never `transition: all`.
- **The layer**: one signature move, done well (masked headline, shutter image, scaling wordmark, pinned story, index with image preview, curtain footer, slow crossfade, edge-to-edge marquee, drag row, colour by chapter; pick by subject, and if the move says nothing about the subject the page is better without it); an entrance for the parts of each section in reading order, staggered 60 to 90ms, rising 16 to 32px with opacity, once, varied by content rather than one preset stamped everywhere; feedback under the hand (press, hover, focus, a menu opening from where it was clicked); one moving picture. The installed skill fails a page with fewer than eight moving elements; that floor was added to make agents do the work and the study itself has strong sites that barely move, so take the structure, not the count.
- **Content is visible without JavaScript**: the hidden pre-animation state applies only under `html.js`, set by a one-line inline script; nothing stays at `opacity: 0` if an observer fails. Reveal on scroll with `IntersectionObserver` (`rootMargin: '0px 0px -12% 0px'`, `threshold: 0.1`, `unobserve` after the first fire), never a scroll listener, never replayed.
- **Recipes worth knowing**: a masked line reveal needs `padding-bottom: 0.08em; margin-bottom: -0.08em` on the mask or descenders clip; an accordion animates `grid-template-rows: 0fr` to `1fr` and never measures height; scroll-linked drift lives inside `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` with travel under 8% of the element; a pinned story uses `position: sticky`, never scroll hijacking; menus open from the trigger with `transform-origin`, nothing enters from `scale(0)` (start at 0.95 to 0.98), only a centred dialog scales from its centre.
- **Hover** goes behind `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` because touch fires hover on tap and leaves it stuck; hover raises contrast; no weight change; not every card lifts.
- **Reduced motion means fewer and gentler, not none**: keep opacity and colour changes, drop travel, scale, parallax and autoplay; write recipes inside `no-preference` so the reduced state is the finished state. Loops pause off screen and when the tab is hidden. `will-change` only on the element about to animate, removed after. No scroll hijacking, no smooth-scroll library by default, no custom cursor: they cost usability, 30% of the score and the line most winners score lowest on.
- **Motion tells**: the same fade-up on every block; a dozen staggered entrances in the hero; hover lift on every card; bounce or elastic easing; infinite loops that mean nothing (pulsing dots, floating shapes, shimmering text); a reveal that never fires and leaves a blank band.

## 8. The verify step

1. **Lint and render.** From the installed skill: `node ~/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/lint.mjs <files>` then `node .../scripts/inspect.mjs <url or index.html>`, which renders at 1440, 768 and 390, measures headline line counts, type sizes, loaded fonts, contrast, overflow, covered and clipped text, and saves screenshots. Pass `--allow <rule>` for a rule the brand or the brief overrides, and say so in the report. The rules that are breakage rather than taste and hold for any page: overflow, broken image, covered or clipped text, contrast under 2.6:1 (4.5:1 needed), a declared font that did not load, a headline over two lines, body under 15px, more than three blocks under 13px, two or more near-empty desktop screens, no focal point (largest type under 4.5vw and no picture at 30% of the viewport), no button in the first screen, more than nine font sizes or three families, default link colours, lorem, emoji, `100vh`, `transition: all`, `scale(0)`, stock phrases.
2. **Look at the screenshots**, never the code, and make each pass on its own: one second (what did you see? "a website" means no focal point); squint (two or three masses, or everything medium); cover the logo (could this belong to the competitor, and does the idea reach the footer?); edges (count the left-edge x positions: two or three, or six); space (holes beside short columns); type (a decisive jump body to headline, no orphan, no paragraph past 75 characters); colour (name the proportions); images (at least a few real ones, one treatment, every one doing a job); sameness (one word per section's layout; the same word twice in a row is the template); phone (its own composition or the desktop squeezed); fullness (each desktop screen a composed poster, not a narrow column with dead ground); substance (every section the brief asked for, complete, readable); collisions (anything over a headline at any scroll position, anything off the phone's edge); the competitor and the live thing.
3. **Score like a juror**: design 40%, usability 30%, creativity 20%, content 10%, each 1 to 10. Real Sites of the Day land around 7.5 to 8.0; an honest 8.5 is rare; if your first score for your own work is 9, score it again as the juror who did not build it. Caps: a headline of three lines, an eyebrow, numbering, a decorative gradient, a resting shadow or a visible decorative hairline caps the overall at 6.5; "could belong to anyone" caps creativity at 6; no real button on the first screen caps usability at 7; anything covering a headline caps usability at 6; a blank band or clipped content caps it at 5; a placeholder caps content at 5.
4. **Fix in this order**, because later items cannot rescue earlier ones: the idea; hierarchy (one dominant element per screen); composition (shared edges, section spacing, variety, holes); typography; imagery; colour; detail; motion last. When removing something leaves a gap, close it, enlarge what remains, or let a picture or a field take it; never refill with another ornament.
5. **Three rounds at most**: critique, fix the top three, re-inspect, critique. Stop early when a round finds nothing a stranger would notice. Under 7.5 after round three, say so plainly with the remaining problems; do not round up.

What jurors punished, each now a measurable rule: a quiet page that read as empty; a small headline; text sliding over pictures or cut at the top; a page recoloured mid-section; rows cut at the right edge; a large sentence over three or more lines; navigation that took over the first screen. How the 54 weak pages failed: the category's stock template (about 20); centred headline, pill badge, two buttons, screenshot (about 9); a distinctive hero then stock modules (about 8); a default typeface (about 13); text too small or faint (about 13); an undersized headline and no focal point (about 9); the page never says what the thing is (about 12); thin content stretched over many screens (about 11); gates, preloaders and pop-ups before content (about 9); a phone layout that is the desktop shrunk (about 8). And the faults of the best sites not to copy: 9 to 13px body text, information hidden behind the concept, the idea running out mid-page, text over footage with no scrim.

## 9. Tells, dated

A tell is a device produced by default, everywhere, without a reason. Replacements are procedures (derive from the subject), never products: every published "use this instead" list has become the next wave within months.

| Wave | Tells |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2023 to 2024 | Violet or indigo gradients on hero, buttons and glows; gradient-filled headline text; glass panels; glowing "Get started"; a grey shadow under every card; three identical feature cards with an icon in a rounded square (1% of top sites, 28% of weak); emoji as icons; centred headline, subtitle, two buttons, screenshot below (7% of top sites, 24% of weak) |
| 2025 | Eyebrow or kicker above every heading; a pill badge above the headline ("New", "Beta"); one headline word in italic serif or colour; a bento of rounded tiles as the feature section; near-black with one neon accent and a glow; floating pill navigation with blur; a fake dashboard of grey bars; a pulsing "Available for work" dot; fade-and-rise on every section |
| 2025 to 2026, the remedy that became the look | Yellow-cream ground with terracotta, rust or clay; a soft serif display as the shortcut to "premium"; broadsheet cosplay (hairline rules everywhere, zero radius, dense columns); performative labels ("Field notes", "Quietly trusted by") |
| Structural | A full sentence at display size over three or four lines; the stock section order; every section the same layout family; everything centred (warned over 72%); one medium radius on everything (the 9 to 16px "soft" radius is the weak-site default; top sites 52% square, 23% fully round); nine nearly equal font sizes; invented social proof; a CSS box where a picture belongs |

Removing a tell leaves space. Fill it with scale, with the full content, with a picture made for this subject, or with a flat field; a page with every tell removed and nothing in their place is an empty page with good manners, and that is a tell too.
